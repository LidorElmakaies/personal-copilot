Shabbat, calendar & reminders architecture

A Calendar Service handles all the calendar calculations. A Reminders Service decides when to notify you and enqueues a job on a BullMQ queue (Redis). A Notification Service decides how (push today, and email or SMS later with one new adapter each). A Users Service knows who you are and where you last opened the app; it announces changes on Kafka, so other services keep their own copy instead of asking it.

The services

Phase 1

Calendar Service

Every calendar calculation, in one place.

  • Next Shabbat, month grid, holidays, fasts, daily times
  • Stateless: location in, times out. No database.
  • Used by the phone (via Gateway) and by Reminders
Phase 2 · was Auth

Users Service

How you log in, and who you are.

  • The Auth Service, renamed: register, login, tokens, account as before
  • New profiles table: first and last name, phone (all optional), and your location, keyed by your user id (the token's sub)
  • Account and profile are created in one transaction, so nothing has to be synced
  • Every profile change is announced as your full state on users.user-state; delete account (password required, immediate) on users.user-deleted
Phase 2

Reminders Service

What to remind you about, and when.

  • Every reminder belongs to one user and needs you to be logged in
  • First type: before candle lighting, with the offset you pick (e.g. 1h 30m), repeating every week
  • Later: other types (before a holiday, custom date/time) reuse the same table
  • When a reminder is due it enqueues a job and stops there. It never knows about push, email or SMS.
Phase 2

Notification Service

How to reach you.

  • Processes notification-requested jobs from the queue, sending to each of your devices and retrying the job for any it couldn't reach
  • Knows your browser push subscriptions (later: email address, phone number)
  • One adapter per channel behind INotificationChannel. Email or SMS is a new adapter, with no change to Reminders.
  • Any future service can send you a notification the same way

Where it lives: a bell button on the Shabbat section of the Home card (H1). Tapping it opens a sheet with an hours/minutes picker and quick presets. The button label shows what's set. It's per user, so if you're signed out, tapping it shows the same "log in to use this" prompt the Account tab uses.

What gets saved: one row per user and type, not one per week.

How it fires: no polling: each enabled reminder has one BullMQ job delayed until next_fire_at. When it runs, Reminders enqueues notification-requested, then asks Calendar for next week's candle lighting at your location, sets next_fire_at = that − 90 min and schedules the next delayed job. Change the offset, or move more than 5 km, and the pending job is replaced right away.

The queue job

// queue: notification-requested   dedupe id: notificationId
{
  "notificationId": "7f3c…",      // job dedupe id
  "userId": "u_123",
  "title": "Shabbat in 1h 30m",
  "body": "Candle lighting at 18:04",
  "channels": ["webpush"],        // optional; omitted = every channel
  "source": "reminders",
  "requestedAt": "2026-10-02T13:34:00Z",
  "expiresAt": "2026-10-02T15:04:00Z"       // required; never sent after this
}

This lives in libs/queue-contracts, so both services share one typed contract.

notificationId is the job's dedupe id until expiresAt, so enqueueing the same notification twice queues it once. Every publisher enqueues it with the same options (notificationRequestedPublishOptions): 8 attempts, backoff doubling from 30 s. The Notification Service sends to each of your devices and records the ones done in the job's progress; if the push service was busy or unreachable for some, the job is retried and only those are tried again. A device can rarely get a notification twice, but a transient error never loses one.

The message says what to tell you, not where. That's why adding email later is only a change to the Notification Service.

Events (Kafka)

Kafka for events, BullMQ for jobs. An event says something happened, and any number of services may care; each reads it with its own consumer group, at its own pace, and catches up after a restart. A job is work done once, possibly delayed or in parallel: notification-requested and a reminder's next firing stay on BullMQ.

TopicPublished byCarriesRead by
users.user-deletedUsersuserIdReminders, Notifications (each deletes its own data about you)
users.user-stateUsersyour full state: name, phone, location, version. Keyed by user id, compacted; an empty message when you're deleted.Reminders (location). A future SMS channel can start reading it any time.

No lost events (outbox). The Users Service saves the change and the event in the same database transaction; a small relay publishes saved events to Kafka. A crash or a Kafka outage only delays an event.

Full state, not changes. Each users.user-state message is the whole picture with a version number, so a duplicate or late message can't undo a newer one. Because the topic is compacted, Kafka keeps at least the latest message per user, so a service that starts listening later still gets everyone.

Existing accounts start without a profile; one is created on their first profile write (name, phone or location).

Your exact location

expo-location gives GPS coordinates, and @hebcal/core calculates from latitude, longitude and time zone directly. The time zone also decides Israel vs. abroad (one day of Yom Tov or two). Candle lighting is 20 minutes before sunset in Israel and 18 abroad; city customs (Jerusalem 40, Haifa 30) become a setting later.

Needs HTTPS in the browser. Phone browsers only allow GPS on HTTPS, so tailscale serve puts the app and Gateway behind a real certificate for the PC's *.ts.net name (devops/tailscale/serve.sh). The native app doesn't need this.

Follows your phone. Reminders fire while the app is closed, so the server keeps your location, in the Users Service. When you're signed in, the phone sends it only if you moved more than 5 km since the last one it sent, or your time zone changed. A web app can't read GPS in the background, so this is where you last opened the app. Two phones in two places: the last update wins. Signed out, Home uses the phone's GPS directly.

Build order

Phase 1 · Shabbat + Calendar tab
  1. Calendar Service: apps/calendar with /calendar/shabbat, and @hebcal/core behind ICalendarCalculator. Tests against known dates and places.
  2. Gateway calendar-proxy: same shape as auth-proxy, rate-limited.
  3. Devops: a compose entry with no published port; HTTPS through tailscale serve.
  4. Home (H1): location permission, locationSlice, calendarSlice, times and countdown under the clock, last result cached.
  5. Calendar tab (C1): /calendar/month, the grid with both dates, and the day card.
Phase 2 · Reminders + notifications
  1. Queue contract: notification-requested in libs/queue-contracts, BullMQ client in libs/queue-client.
  2. Notification Service: the queue consumer, push subscriptions, and the Web Push adapter (encrypted payload). Subscriptions are only accepted for known browser push services (Google FCM, Mozilla, Apple, Microsoft), so the server never POSTs anywhere else, and every notification carries an expiresAt: it's skipped if the service only gets to it after that, and the push service drops it if the phone can't be reached before then. Devices the push service couldn't reach are retried with backoff until expiresAt, one job per notification.
  3. Reminders Service: Postgres and the reminder API.
  4. Kafka for events: the broker back, libs/kafka-client with an outbox, contracts for the three topics.
  5. Auth becomes the Users Service: the rename first, then profiles (name, phone, location), delete account, and the two events.
  6. Reminders and Notifications listen: Reminders keeps your location from users.user-state; both clean up on users.user-deleted.
  7. Frontend account + location: name and phone at register and on the Account tab, delete account, location sent after a real move.
  8. Reminder scheduler: BullMQ delayed jobs, moved when the offset or your location changes.
  9. Frontend reminders: a service worker + "Turn on notifications", the bell button and offset sheet on the Home card, remindersSlice.
