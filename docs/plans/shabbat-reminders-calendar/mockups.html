How it behaves

  • Drag sideways: the grid moves with your finger, the next or previous month coming in beside it. Swipe left for the next month, right for the previous one, like turning a page.
  • Let go past a quarter of the width, or with a quick flick: it slides the rest of the way (about 250 ms, easing out). Otherwise it springs back.
  • The arrows run the same slide, so both feel the same.
  • Up/down still scrolls the screen: the swipe only takes over once the finger moves more sideways than down.
  • The new month slides in with its day numbers straight away (the phone knows those); the Hebrew dates and dots fade in when the server's answer arrives. Here the answer is held back 0.4 s to show it.
  • The day card moves to today when you swipe back to this month, otherwise to the 1st; it fades rather than slides.
  • Hold and keep dragging to run through several months in one go: each month you pass hands over to the next. The title follows the month mostly in view, and letting go lands on the nearest one (or the next in the flick's direction).
  • The two months either side of the one shown are loaded ahead (task 3.3d), so a swipe lands on a month already filled in; numbers-first only further out.
  • Today appears in the header whenever you're away from today (another month or day); it slides back and selects today.

How it behaves

  • Drag sideways anywhere on a screen: the screen moves with your finger and the next or previous tab comes in beside it. Swipe left for the tab to the right in the bar, right for the one to the left. Same feel as the months (SwipePager): past a quarter of the width or a quick flick, it slides the rest of the way (about 250 ms, easing out); otherwise it springs back.
  • The bar follows: the highlighted tab is the one mostly in view, so it changes halfway through the drag, like the month title.
  • Tapping a tab runs the same slide. A jump of more than one tab (Home → Admin) doesn't blur through the ones between: the tab next to the target is put in place first, then the target slides in, as the Today button does for months.
  • First and last tab: dragging past Home, or past the last tab, moves the screen only a little (a third of the finger's distance) and it springs back on release. No wrap-around.
  • Calendar's month grid keeps swiping months, exactly as in S1 above. Here it only wiggles and says which way it would go. A swipe that starts anywhere else on Calendar (the month header, the day card) changes tabs. Turn on "Show edge zones" to see the grid's area outlined.
  • Signed out: Account is still in the swipe order. Dragging toward it shows its "log in" notice (the screen you'd get from a direct link). Letting go past the threshold doesn't land there: it springs back and asks "Log in to view Account?", the same prompt as tapping the tab. Turn off "Signed in" to try it.
  • Admin is in the swipe order only for admins. Turn off "Admin" and the last tab is Account.
  • Android's back gesture starts within about 20 dp of either side edge. A drag that starts there is left to Android and never moves the tabs. "Show edge zones" shades them; here a drag from a zone just says "Back (Android)".
  • Up/down still scrolls each screen: the swipe only takes over once the finger moves more sideways than down (10 px either way, as the months).
  • Every tab is kept ready so its neighbour is already drawn when a drag reaches it: Home, Calendar and Account load their data at app start rather than on first visit (Admin only for admins). Each screen keeps its scroll position.
  • Web gets the same: a mouse or touch drag, and the bar.

Decided (2026-10-19): signed out, Account stays in the swipe order and a swipe onto it springs back and asks to log in, as here; Calendar needs no login (its own reminders will, task 3.6). The slide stays at 250 ms, as the months.

R

How it behaves (both)

  • Add opens a bottom sheet (the candle-lighting sheet's BottomSheet): title, the date it starts (the day tapped, ‹ › to move it), the time (hours, then minutes in 5s), and how it repeats: Once · Daily · Monthly · Yearly.
  • Monthly and yearly ask which calendar: Gregorian ("the 19th", "every 19 October") or Hebrew ("the 8th", "every 8 Cheshvan"). Under it, the next three dates in both calendars, so you can see a Hebrew birthday moving around the Gregorian year.
  • Odd dates get a note saying what happens, e.g. a 30 Cheshvan in a year Cheshvan has 29 days, a 31st, 29 Feb, Adar in a leap year (see the open questions below).
  • Each reminder in the list (day card or "Show all") shows its time and how it repeats; tap it to edit in the same sheet. Edits change the whole series. Delete is in the edit sheet and asks first.
  • On the grid: every day a reminder falls on gets the amber count (task 3.3c). A daily reminder counts on every day from its start.
  • A once-reminder that's gone off stays on its day, dimmed.
  • Signed out: no reminders anywhere; Add asks "Log in to add reminders?" (ConfirmModal, as the candle-lighting bell). Try the "Signed in" switch above.
  • Title: English or Hebrew letters, digits and basic punctuation, up to 60 characters (task 3.6c); Save stays off until there's a title.

Drag a reminder's row to the left: a red Delete appears and stays if you pass half of it. Tap it and the reminder is gone, with Undo in the toast for 4 s instead of a question. A tap on the row still opens the edit sheet (which has Delete too).

X3

Tap → details, then Edit or Delete

Tapping a reminder shows it first: the time, how it repeats and its next dates in both calendars, with Edit and Delete. Edit opens the edit sheet; Delete asks once.

  • See the next dates without risking a change
  • One more tap to edit

Repeating reminders (the switch above, on to start). For a repeating reminder (daily, monthly, yearly) Save and Delete first ask "Only Mon 19 Oct" or "Every time". "Only" on delete skips that one day (its count goes from the grid); on an edit it moves that day to a once-reminder of its own, and the series skips it. A once-reminder never asks. Switch it off to see the other way: every edit or delete is for the whole series. It needs exceptions stored per reminder (task 3.6c) and the scheduler to skip them (3.6d).

Decided (2026-10-19)

The rules the sheet's notes follow, for task 3.6b
  1. Where Add lives: P2, the + button over the calendar.
  2. A date a month or year doesn't have (a Hebrew 30th in a 29-day month, the 31st, 29 Feb): the last day of that month, never skipped.
  3. Hebrew yearly in Adar, made in a regular year: Adar II in a leap year (the birthday rule). Made in Adar I or Adar II of a leap year: Adar in a regular year.
  4. Yahrzeit: no separate option for now; Yearly · Hebrew covers it.
  5. Daily reminders count on every day of the grid, as shown.
  6. Editing and deleting: X1, the edit sheet with Delete in it.
  7. Repeating reminders always ask "Only this day" or "Every time" before an edit or a delete is saved; a once-reminder never asks.

Mix and match

C2's agenda and C1's grid can be one screen with a List / Month switch at the top. H1 or H2 fit any calendar choice.

Where the times come from

Candle lighting, Havdalah and holidays are calculated from your coordinates and date. A small backend service behind Gateway (or on-device with @hebcal/core) can do this.

Reminders later

Every design leaves a slot for reminders: the day card (C1), inline amber items (C2), or a per-day list (C3). Push delivery comes with that feature.
